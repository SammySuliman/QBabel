Everyone starts with the same neutral basis. Only received utterances and their measured readings change it. There are no basis controls.

What can I say?

This prototype parses a small explicit English grammar. Use Alice, Bob, or Charlie with help, trust, follow, call, or see. For example: “Alice helps Bob.”; “Yesterday, Alice helped Bob.”; “Tomorrow, Bob will help Alice.”; “Alice did not help Bob.”

Conversation phrases: “I agree.”, “I disagree.”, “Who?”, “When?”, “Did it happen?”, “Say that again.” These update listeners’ frames without asserting a new event.

Try Alice: “Alice helps Bob.” → Bob: “When?” → Charlie: “I disagree.” → Alice: “Alice helps Bob.” Compare the repeated sentence after different histories.

    Inspect the rules and quantum state
    Each listener gets four qubits: agency (written subject / reversed roles), tense (past / future), polarity (positive / negative), and surface order (SVO / VSO). Agency and word order are separate. VSO readings include explicit role labels to keep them interpretable.
    English words prepare amplitudes. The speaker’s stored frame transforms the intent state. A coherent controlled-phase gate links the two listeners’ agency qubits according to shared history. Each listener then applies the inverse of their own accumulated basis gates before measurement. Received messages and outcomes update that basis for the next exchange.
    These update rules are invented grammar, not a learned model of natural conversation. Bases use noncommuting Y and Z rotations. Every utterance prepares a fresh quantum state; histories retain classical basis transformations, not persistent unmeasured physical qubits.
    

    A quantum-inspired constructed language prototype, not a factual claim about events or a model of psychology. No external NLP service. Local measurements use exact quantum probabilities with classical sampling; Qollab backend results use Qiskit.
